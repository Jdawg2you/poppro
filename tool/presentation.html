How this line is built, and why in that order. Three deliberate choices, because this is the most emotionally loaded sentence in the script and it has a named relative in it.
1. The person and the statistic are kept apart. “Your dad, at 58” followed straight by a mortality figure reads as your dad died and you are next. The sentence between them — “I’m not going to make more of that than it deserves” — is doing real work: it is what separates naming a true risk from frightening someone with their own family. Do not cut it to save four seconds.
2. The family history is the reason; the statistic is only context. The line says the cover is there because of their history, and the figure sits underneath as scale. Reversed — statistic first, as the argument — it becomes a fear close.
3. “On purpose, not as an upsell” is there because the client is about to be asked for more money, and naming that plainly is what keeps the previous sentence from sounding like the set-up for it.
4. The relative is spoken, but not in the format it is stored in. This moved twice. It was briefly an agent cue only, because fam.who was free text an agent typed — shorthand, and able to name a heart attack one sentence after the line said cancer. Your change of 17 October replaced that box with clicks, so it is now generated from the same answers as fam.history and the two cannot disagree. The relative is therefore safe to say — but not as stored: “your mom (cancer); your dad (cancer)” has brackets and a semicolon in it and repeats the condition the sentence just named. So the spoken line takes fam.rel — “your mom and dad” — and the detailed version stays in the cue below for when they ask which side it was. The defusing sentence still sits between the relative and the figure, which was the point of the original ordering.7.5

Most people do. These aren't high-deductible plans — they're defined-benefit, paying a set amount from day one. Can I walk you through a real example?

“Why not just stick with the ACA plan?”

People feel that way, especially with a subsidy. What they found is narrow networks, high deductibles and a rate rise every year. This renews at the same price, on the First Health PPO. Want to see it side by side?

“I've never heard of ManhattanLife.”

Fair — not a household name. Around since 1850, one of the oldest insurers in the country. Clients found the private PPO through First Health gave them more control and fewer restrictions. Want to see their AM Best rating and the network?

“I'm healthy, I don't need insurance.”

That's exactly why now is the time. People felt that way until an accident or a surprise bill. Being healthy is what locks in this rate and holds it to 65. Makes sense to protect your good health while it's cheapest?

“Can you just send me some information?”

Absolutely. What clients found is that reading it alone didn't show them what they personally qualify for. I'll send it and walk you through it in five minutes — this evening or tomorrow morning?
