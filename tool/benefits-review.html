Coaching notes — agent only, never on screen for a client

What this call has to do

  • Pass the baton. Get them logged in, registered and installed on this call. A client who did will call ManhattanLife; one who did not will call you, for everything. Every registration done now is one less call later.
  • Teach them to fish. You are the broker, not the carrier: they should leave knowing who solves each problem.
  • It is the lapse-prevention call. The premium, the draft date and the account are said in section 2 and nowhere else. Most first-month “lapses” are a first draft that bounced.
  • Say “First Health,” not “Aetna.” That is what a front desk searches for.
  • Close with the referral ask — they are grateful and nothing has gone wrong yet — and the annual review: name the month out loud.
  • Fifteen minutes each. Eight policies a month is two hours that removes most of your support burden.

Say the figure and the date early, plainly, and only once. It is the single most common reason a good case lapses in its first month, and this call is your one chance to put the number in their head before the bank does.
Custom draft dates die in the handoff. If this client asked for a draft date that is not the effective date — a Social Security benefit week, a payday — do not say “usually that same day”.
