# TRIAD with Cigna PPO — product reference
Pulled 2026-09-24 from `1enrollment.com/information/product.cfm?page=45437&id=994524`
(public page, no login). Rates cross-checked against `Rate Sheet - Triad 2026.pdf` — they match.

**Positioning: this is the Cadillac.** A real PPO network with a real out-of-pocket maximum and
an unlimited lifetime benefit — the thing a ManhattanLife fixed-benefit package structurally
cannot offer. When someone qualifies and can afford it, it is the best coverage on the tree
short of a subsidised marketplace plan.

## THE GATE — check this before you price anything

> **Applicant MUST have their own EIN. Applicant MUST be self-employed.**
> Employer-paid is not allowed for single-household applicants.
> Employer-sponsored with **2 or more W2 employees** goes through the USAHP **Group Major
> Medical** listing instead, not this one.

This is a 1099 product. It disqualifies more of Jesse's book than the health questions do:

## How it actually works

- **Self-insured group policy. Cigna is the network, not the carrier.** Calling Cigna "the
  insurance company" is wrong and it is the kind of thing that comes back.
- ⚠️ **The two sources name different reinsurers.** The product page says *"For 2026, Fidelity &
  Security Life provides reinsurance to the group."* `Rate Sheet - Triad 2026.pdf` says
  *"Self-Insured Plan — Reinsured by Sirius Point Ltd."* **Don't state either as fact to a
  client** until USAHP confirms which is current for 2026. Most likely the rate sheet carries
  forward last year's footer, but that is a guess.
- **Individually underwritten. Approval is not guaranteed.**
- **Deductible and max out-of-pocket always run calendar year, Jan 1 – Dec 31**, no matter when
  the member goes effective. A June start means a full deductible for half a year, then it
  resets. This matters enormously for a mid-year enrolment and it is easy to miss.
- **Rate locked for one year from the effective date.** Coverage offer is one year.
- **$35 underwriting administration fee** at checkout.
- **SEP required to add dependents mid-year.**

## Enrolment timing — a hard monthly cadence

1. Application for underwriting must be submitted **by the 15th**.
2. If approved, the member must **sign to accept the final offer by the 25th**.
3. **Failure to sign the final offer means no coverage.** Not a delay — no coverage.

Build the follow-up around those two dates or the case dies on a calendar technicality.

## Rates are estimates, and the spread is wide

The published rates are **pre-underwritten averages**. Final approved rates land within:

| Tier | Spread from the quoted rate |
|---|---|
| Member only | ± $100 / month |
| Member + spouse | ± $200 / month |
| Member + child | ± $200 / month |
| Member + family | ± $300 / month |

⚠️ The rate sheet adds a qualifier the web page leaves out: **"These ranges are most consistent
for plan deductibles $3,500 – $7,350."** On the 1000, 1500 and 2500 plans the spread can be
wider than the table suggests. Quote those with more room, not less.

**Quote the range, never the point.** A member-only 60-64 plan quoted at $1,157.80 can come back
anywhere from roughly $1,058 to $1,258. Promising the table number is how you lose a case at
the signing stage.

**Two things in that grid worth knowing:**

- **The 1000 Copay plan has the lowest max out-of-pocket of all eight — $5,000.** Lowest
  deductible *and* lowest ceiling. It is the genuinely richest plan on the sheet, not just the
  most expensive.
- **The 7350 runs deductible + 0%** on hospital, surgery, labs and outpatient. Once the
  deductible is met it pays everything. For someone who can carry $7,350 but wants certainty
  above it, that is a real structure and it is the cheapest plan in the range.

Mental health **is** covered here — $30–$50 copay. That is a genuine differentiator against a
ManhattanLife fixed-benefit package, which excludes it.

## Checking the network before you promise a doctor

Cigna PPO directory, no login needed:
1. Select **"Employer or School"**
2. Enter ZIP, select provider type
3. **Continue as Guest**
4. Enter ZIP again, continue
5. Choose **"PPO, Choice Fund PPO"**

This is a different network from First Health, which is what the ManhattanLife lines use. If a
case has both, check both.

## Where this sits on the decision tree

`tracks.md` puts USA Health / Cigna Triad at rung 4, below ManhattanLife, IronHealth and LifeX.
That ordering is about the ManhattanLife package being the default, not about Triad being worse
coverage — **it is better coverage.** It is lower on the tree because it is underwritten, it is
expensive, and it requires the 1099 gate.

**The Kilari lesson stands:** on a family of four in GA, every Triad design priced *above* the
unsubsidised gold marketplace plan, so it stayed in agent notes and never reached the client.
**Price it before you promise it.** Against an unsubsidised marketplace plan it is often
competitive; against a subsidised one it rarely is.

# What Triad actually covers — from the official SBCs

All eight Summary of Benefits documents and the EBA Underwriting Guide are now filed in this
folder, pulled 2026-09-24 from `enrollment123.com/media/1350/SBC's 2026/`. Figures below are
from the **3500 plan**; the structure is the same across the range, the percentages move.

## It is real major medical, and that is the headline

**Maternity is covered.** Office visits, childbirth/delivery professional services and
childbirth/delivery facility services all at **20% after deductible** in network.

**Mental health, behavioral health and substance abuse are covered.** Outpatient at a **$45
copay per visit**; inpatient at **20% after deductible**.

⚠️ **This contradicts the standard package pitch.** On the Scarpinato discovery call the framing
was *"we're not gonna have a plan that would include pregnancy… we're also not gonna have a plan
that covers any sort of major mental medical needs — rehab, extreme medications for mental
concerns."* That is accurate for a **ManhattanLife fixed-benefit package**. It is **not** true of
Triad. Don't carry that line across to a Triad presentation — it undersells the product and it
is wrong.

**Out-of-network is genuinely covered** — deductible then **40% coinsurance**, subject to the
plan's allowable fee. A real PPO with out-of-network benefits, not a narrow network.

## Excluded — the list that matters

- Acupuncture
- Bariatric surgery
- Cosmetic surgery
- **Dental care (adult)**
- **Hearing aids (adult)**
- Infertility treatment
- Long-term care
- Non-emergency care outside the U.S.
- Private-duty nursing
- Routine foot care
- **Weight loss programs**

**Adult dental and adult hearing are excluded**, so a DVH policy still has a job alongside Triad
— that is an add-on sale, not a duplication.

**Weight loss programs are excluded.** Relevant wherever a GLP-1 is prescribed for weight loss
rather than for diabetes; expect that to be a coverage question, not an assumption.

## Covered, with limits

Chiropractic care · durable medical equipment · **hearing aids under 18** · **routine adult eye
care**. So Triad includes some adult vision, which changes the calculus on adding a vision rider.

## Precertification is a real penalty, not a formality

**Failure to obtain precertification reduces the benefit by 50%, up to a $2,500 maximum
penalty.** Say this plainly at the point of sale. It applies to inpatient admissions and it is
the kind of thing a client discovers at the worst possible moment.
